Overview

A delivery date prediction service for a D2C furniture brand. Replaces the fixed "10 to 14 days" promise with a personalized date per product and pincode.

The problem

The brand sells large items — sofas, beds, dining tables — out of three warehouses, through several carriers, with some products on regular production batches and others made to order. Today every customer sees the same fixed delivery promise of "10 to 14 days" regardless of where they live or what they're buying.

That promise is wrong in both directions. A customer in a city with a warehouse next door could have had the item in 4 days and buys from a faster competitor instead. A customer waiting on a delayed production batch actually waits 25 days and leaves a bad review. The brand loses on both sides.

What the system does

The Delivery Estimate Service exposes one endpoint that, given a product and a customer pincode, returns a delivery date along with a confidence level, a human-readable reason, and a breakdown of how the date was computed.

It does this by joining three data sources at request time: live inventory (which warehouse has the product now), scheduled production batches (when out-of-stock items will land, and from which supplier), and historical delivery records (how long deliveries actually take on each lane).

The decision tree

For any request, the service walks down a four-branch decision tree and returns the first branch that applies.

The four cases

CaseWhen it firesWhat the customer sees
In stock nearby Stock at a warehouse in the customer's own region A single date, high confidence
In stock farther Stock available, but only at a warehouse outside the customer's region A single date, high confidence, slightly later
Awaiting batch Out of stock, but a production batch is scheduled with a known supplier A date range sized by supplier reliability, medium or low confidence
Soft fallback / not serviceable Out of stock with no scheduled batch, or pincode in an unserved region A wide range or a clear "not yet available" message

What's deliberately out of scope

The system runs on-demand compute with a short cache, backed by nightly-refreshed statistics. That's a deliberate starting point for this scale of brand. The architecture has obvious next steps for higher traffic: precomputed hot-sets, event-driven cache invalidation, and a learned model in place of bucket statistics. None are worth building until the dashboard says they are. See Technical for details.
