Algorithm

How the system turns inventory state and historical delivery times into a date the customer can trust.

Inputs and outputs

The service takes two inputs:

  • Product — identifies the SKU and (transitively) its supplier and weight class.
  • Pincode — the customer's delivery pincode.

And returns:

  • A delivery date (single date for in-stock, date range for batch-await).
  • A confidence level — high, medium, or low.
  • A human-readable reason explaining the date.
  • A breakdown showing each component of the total (handling, transit, supplier slip).
  • A serviceable flag — false when the pincode isn't in the delivery network.

The decision tree

Branches are evaluated top-down. The first branch whose condition is true returns the response. There is no fall-through and no branch ever returns null.

#ConditionResultConfidence
0 Pincode can't be resolved to any known cluster (no prefix match at all) "Not yet available" — no date —
1 Stock available at a warehouse in the customer's region; lane has statistics Single date High
2 Stock available only at warehouses outside the customer's region Single date (slightly later) High
3 Stock available but no lane statistics yet (new warehouse or new region) Single date using platform default transit Medium
4 Out of stock; production batch scheduled; supplier reliability known Date range Medium (reliable supplier) or Low (flaky)
5 Out of stock; batch scheduled; supplier has no history yet Wider date range (default slip) Low
6 Out of stock; no batch scheduled either 3-to-5 week range (soft fallback) Low

The formulas

For in-stock items

delivery_date = today
              + handling_buffer            (default 1 day)
              + P80(transit_days_for_lane) (from history)

For out-of-stock items with a scheduled batch

earliest = batch_promised_date
         + handling_buffer
         + P80(transit_days_for_lane)

latest   = earliest + P80(supplier_slip_days)

Where P80(x) means: sort the historical values for x and return the value at the 80th percentile.

Why the 80th percentile, not the average

For any delivery lane, transit times follow a right-skewed distribution: most deliveries land in a tight cluster, with a tail of slower ones caused by weather, festivals, or carrier hiccups.

Promised valueCustomers who beat itCustomers who missTypical miss
The mean (~P50)~50%~50%1–8 days late
P80~80%~20%1–2 days late
P95~95%~5%2–5 days late, but every promise is much longer

P80 balances both ends. Most customers get their order on or before the promised date; the few who don't miss by only a day or two. The percentile is a single tunable knob in settings.DELIVERY_PROMISE_PERCENTILE.

Why a date range for out-of-stock items

In-stock orders have one source of uncertainty — the carrier. That converges quickly because we ship thousands of orders on each lane.

Out-of-stock orders waiting on a production batch have a second source of uncertainty: the supplier. A reliable supplier slips by 1 or 2 days against their promised batch date. A flaky one slips by 7 or more.

Collapsing that into a single false-confidence date discards information the brand can use to differentiate between products. The range width is the supplier's reliability made visible to the customer. They self-select: a customer who really wants the sofa will accept a 5-day range; one with a tight deadline will look for a different product.

Pincode resolution

India has roughly 150,000 pincodes. Tracking delivery statistics per individual pincode would need years of data per zone to be statistically meaningful. So pincodes are grouped into delivery clusters — about 2,000 zones with similar transit characteristics.

For pincodes that aren't in the explicit mapping, the resolver tries progressively wider prefix matches:

StepMatch scopeExampleConfidence impact
1Exact pincode110001 → Delhi NCRnone
2First 3 digits (district)110099 → Delhi NCRnone
3First 2 digits (state)11xxxx → nearest mapped clusterone notch down
4First 1 digit (region)1xxxxx → any northern clustertwo notches down
—No match anywhere880001 (Tripura, no warehouse coverage)"not yet available"

The principle: refusing to commit just because the exact pincode is new would lose sales for no good reason. A pincode in the same district as ones we already serve is almost certainly in the same delivery zone. A pincode in a region with no warehouse coverage at all is genuinely not serviceable, and saying so honestly beats pretending with low confidence.

Picking the warehouse

When multiple warehouses have stock, the picker selects the one with the lowest P80 transit time to the customer's cluster. When two warehouses tie — which happens when both lanes fall back to the platform default in the absence of historical data — the warehouse in the customer's own region wins. This is a defensive tiebreaker so Mumbai customers never get their order shipped from Bangalore just because the database returned the rows in that order.

Confidence levels

LevelMeansWhen it's used
High The system has solid statistical evidence for this date Stock at any warehouse with a healthy lane (10+ recent deliveries)
Medium The system has partial evidence; the date is a calibrated guess Stock with no lane history yet, or batch arriving with a known reliable supplier
Low The system is filling a gap; treat the range as widely as shown Out-of-stock with a flaky supplier, region-level pincode match, or soft fallback
